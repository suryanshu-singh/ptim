# Prior-Art and Novelty Assessment for PTIM (Predictive Tool Interference Modeling / Tool-Space Risk Predictor)

## TL;DR
- The specific predictive framing you propose — a learned, feature-based regressor that estimates the marginal behavioral damage of adding a *specific unseen tool* to an LLM agent's catalog **without executing the post-addition workload** — appears genuinely open as of August 2026: a targeted negative-result search found no paper doing true ex-ante prediction on unseen tools. But almost everything *surrounding* that slot (tool-space interference, causal filtering, set-level/higher-order interaction modeling, ex-ante certification) is already heavily occupied.
- Of your three claimed contributions, only **(i) pre-deployment prediction of marginal degradation from an unseen tool** is defensibly novel; **(ii) predicting tool-selection distribution shift** and **(iii) the structural interaction model** are largely anticipated by HYSET, ToolChoiceConfusion/CMTF, and the datamodels literature, and are mechanically a transfer of existing machinery to a new object.
- Recommendation: pursue this as a tightly scoped empirical paper plus an open benchmark, **not** as a grand theory of tool interference. The single highest-priority experiment is the cheap-baseline falsification — does embedding cosine similarity between the candidate tool and the existing catalog already predict most of the degradation delta? If yes, the contribution collapses; run that **first**, before investing in the full counterfactual dataset.

## Key Findings

### 1. The named neighboring works almost all exist and are correctly characterized — with important nuances.

- **Microsoft Research "tool-space interference" — REAL.** A Microsoft Research AI Frontiers effort led by research engineer Tyler Payne: a blog ("Tool-space interference in the MCP era: Designing for agent compatibility at scale"), a video, and an open-source CLI tool ("MCP Interviewer") that analyzes live MCP servers for descriptive statistics (tool count, schema depth/length), builds functional test plans with GPT-4.1, and grades schemas against rubrics. It is **descriptive/diagnostic measurement of observed servers**, not prediction of an unseen tool's effect. It coins the term you build on and is the closest "framing" predecessor, but it does not predict marginal degradation.

- **HYSET — REAL, and a significant threat to contributions (ii) and (iii).** "Tools Are Not Islands: Set-Level Tool Retrieval for LLM Agents via Query-Conditioned Hyperedge Prediction" (arXiv 2607.25718). Formulates tool retrieval as query-conditioned hyperedge prediction on a tool co-invocation hypergraph and explicitly models **cardinality-specific interactions** so that "tool compatibility can vary with set size." Its F_set is parameterized as a sum over pairs but "can represent structured joint effects at orders up to M," and it transfers to held-out tools and categories. This directly occupies your "learned interference graph / pairwise + higher-order interaction decomposition" claim — though it targets *retrieval* (which subset to show), not *predicting the degradation caused by an addition*.

- **CMTF / GIST-CMTF — REAL, correctly named.** "ToolChoiceConfusion: Causal Minimal Tool Filtering for Reliable LLM Agents" (arXiv 2606.06284, Rahul Suresh Babu et al.) proposes training-free causal filtering using precondition-effect contracts to expose only the minimal next-step causal frontier; evaluated on 102 tasks, 100 tools, four LLM backends, 2,448 runs. "GIST-CMTF" (arXiv 2606.16813) adds goal-state inference (97.0% task success vs. 80.1% for top-goal CMTF). A companion "Contract2Tool" (arXiv 2606.07904) learns per-tool preconditions/effects. These are **runtime filtering** methods that act on the present tool set; they do not predict the ex-ante effect of an unseen candidate. Their "causality" is causal *sufficiency* for task progress, not treatment-effect (ATE/ITE) estimation of a catalog addition — so your "tool exposure as causal treatment" framing is thematically adjacent but distinct.

- **ToolScope — REAL.** "ToolScope: Enhancing LLM Agent Tool Use through Tool Merging and Context-Aware Filtering" (arXiv 2510.20036; ACL 2026 long paper; Marianne Menglin Liu, D. Garcia, F. Parllaku, V. Upadhyay, S. F. A. Shah, D. Roth). ToolScopeMerger auto-audits/fixes redundant tool merges; ToolScopeRetriever ranks/selects top-k. Reported gains of 8.38%–38.6% in tool-selection accuracy — specifically 34.6% on Seal-Tools, 38.6% on UltraTool, and 8.8% on BFCL. Redundancy-reduction/pruning on observed tools; not prediction.

- **MCPEvol-Bench — REAL.** arXiv 2607.14642 (Huanxi Liu et al.). 123 multi-version MCP servers, 11 mutation operators simulating tool evolution, 201 tasks, 12 LLMs; GPT-5.4 and Claude-Sonnet-4-6 decline 13.7% and 14.4% on evolved servers. It **measures** degradation under tool evolution across three stages; it does not predict which evolution/addition causes degradation. (This is your best ready-made evaluation substrate.)

- **EvolveTool-Bench — REAL but different from what the proposal implies.** GitHub benchmark (abekek/evolvetool-bench) and paper "Beyond Task Completion: A Verification-vs.-Conformance Gap in Tool-Evolving Agents" (arXiv 2604.00392). It diagnoses the **quality of LLM-generated tool libraries** (reuse, redundancy, composition, regression stability, safety) in agents that *create* their own tools — not prediction of interference from an externally added tool.

- **RobustBench-TC — REAL, correctly characterized.** "When Simulation Lies: A Sim-to-Real Benchmark and Domain-Randomized RL Recipe for Tool-Use Agents" (arXiv 2605.11928). 22 perturbation types organized by POMDP component (observation/action/reward-metadata/transition), each grounded in a real GitHub issue; 21 models 1.5B–32B. Measures robustness to tool-interface perturbations; does not predict the effect of adding a tool. (Note: an unrelated adversarial-robustness image benchmark is also called "RobustBench"; the tool one is RobustBench-TC.)

- **"Semantic Tool Discovery" — REAL.** "Semantic Tool Discovery for Large Language Models: A Vector-Based Approach to MCP Tool Selection" (arXiv 2603.20313). Dense-embedding retrieval of 3–5 relevant tools; 121 tools/5 servers; ~99.6% token reduction, 97.1% hit rate at K=3. Standard retrieval — and the field where your cheap-baseline threat lives.

### 2. The single most threatening work for the ex-ante/gating claim is LLMCert-T — but it does NOT predict.
"Quantitative Certification of Agentic Tool Selection" (arXiv 2510.03992; Jehyeok Yeon, Isha Chaudhary, Gagandeep Singh, University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign; v1 Oct 5 2025 under title "Quantifying Distributional Robustness of Agentic Tool-Selection," v2 May 13 2026). It is "the first statistical framework that returns high-confidence upper bounds on the probability that a tool-selection pipeline satisfies a declared safety specification under a realistic tool distribution." Crucially, it "models tool-selection evaluation as a Bernoulli estimation problem," runs *n* trials, and "aggregates the per-trial outcomes into a one-sided Clopper–Pearson upper bound." It "treats the tool-selection pipeline as a black box," generates adversarial inserted tools (homoglyph names, credibility markers like "v2"/"[official]"), and **executes** them. It is empirical, sampling-based, black-box certification — **not** ex-ante feature-based prediction, and it does not model tool interference structurally. It occupies the "pre-deployment risk of tool registries with a gating decision" territory conceptually, but by measurement, leaving the predictive slot open.

### 3. The datamodels / influence-function transfer is real and is your strongest intellectual-honesty risk.
"Datamodels: Predicting Predictions from Training Data" (Ilyas, Park, Engstrom, Leclerc, Madry; arXiv 2202.00622; ICML 2022) is structurally identical to your proposal applied to a different object: it learns a surrogate g(S′) that predicts, from *which subset of training points is included*, the model's output/loss on a target — explicitly "predicting the effect of dataset counterfactuals" and "estimating the causal effect of removing training images." Descendants: DsDm (arXiv 2401.12926); InfICL (arXiv 2402.11750) and DETAIL (NeurIPS 2024) for in-context demonstration selection. RAG-side analogues exist too — retrieval/generation performance prediction (RPP/GPP) and QPP-adapted predictors of RAG gain. **PTIM is, at the machinery level, "a datamodel for the tool catalog."** A knowledgeable reviewer will say so. The novelty is therefore in the *object and its features* (tool schemas/descriptions/name overlap), not the predictive formulation.

### 4. The "established" adjacent phenomena are indeed established — plus two you should cite.
Tool-count degradation is broadly documented: Kate et al., "LongFuncEval" (arXiv 2505.10570, IBM Research) report that "performance drops vary from 7.59% to 85.58% (excluding Mistral-large)" as the catalog grows from 8K to 120K tokens; the Berkeley Function-Calling Leaderboard shows accuracy "dropping from 43% to just 2% on calendar scheduling tasks when the number of tools expanded from 4 to 51." Practitioner reports place a practical "safe zone" at "roughly 10–20 tools per reasoning context... Above 40–50, many production workflows become unreliable" — "a plateau of acceptable performance, followed by a cliff." RAG-MCP (Corca Inc., arXiv 2505.03275) "more than triples tool selection accuracy (43.13% vs 13.62% baseline)." Two works you should explicitly engage: **"Cross-Component Interference in LLM Agent Scaffolding" (arXiv 2605.05716)** already frames negative marginal returns from adding a component across 10 seeds — very close to your "marginal damage of an addition," but for scaffolding, not tools; and **"The 99% Success Paradox" (arXiv 2605.18857, ICLR Blogposts 2026)**, which describes a tool-selection "collapse zone" where a 58-tool setup with 3–5 relevant tools "operates at λ≈4, deep into the collapse zone," and "even a perfect tool selector achieves only ~0.02 bits of selectivity over random chance." Your **phase-transition hypothesis is a plausible but partially-anticipated framing**, not virgin territory. "How Many Tools Should an LLM Agent See? A Chance-Corrected Answer" (arXiv 2605.24660) formalizes shortlist-size tradeoffs.

## Details

### Ranked threat table (highest threat to novelty first)

1. **Datamodels (Ilyas et al., ICML 2022) + descendants — threat to formulation novelty.** Establishes the exact predict-the-counterfactual-of-an-addition machinery; does ex-ante prediction, but on training data. Undermines any claim that the *predictive formulation* is new.
2. **LLMCert-T (arXiv 2510.03992) — threat to the gating/pre-deployment-risk claim.** Owns "certify tool-selection safety under third-party tool insertion with a deployment threshold," but by sampling/execution, black-box, non-predictive of a specific unseen tool. Leaves the predictive slot open.
3. **HYSET (arXiv 2607.25718) — threat to the structural-interaction and distribution claims.** Learned hypergraph model with cardinality-specific higher-order interactions over tools, generalizing to held-out tools. Retrieval, not degradation prediction, but contribution (iii) is largely pre-empted.
4. **ToolChoiceConfusion / CMTF + Contract2Tool (arXiv 2606.06284 / 2606.07904) — threat to the causal framing.** "Tool exposure as a causal problem" is staked out, though not as ATE/ITE of catalog additions.
5. **Microsoft "tool-space interference" + MCP Interviewer — threat to terminology/problem ownership.** Owns the name and the diagnostic-measurement space; not predictive.
6. **Cross-Component Interference (arXiv 2605.05716) — threat to the "marginal damage of an addition" framing.** Measures negative marginal returns of adding components with seed-level rigor; not tools, not predictive.
7. **MCPEvol-Bench (arXiv 2607.14642)** — closest ready-made evaluation substrate; measures evolution-induced degradation; not predictive.
8. **RobustBench-TC (arXiv 2605.11928)** — perturbation taxonomy and feature ideas for interface changes; measurement.
9. **ToolScope (arXiv 2510.20036) / Semantic Tool Discovery (arXiv 2603.20313)** — redundancy merging and retrieval; the embedding-similarity cheap baseline lives here.
10. **"How Many Tools" (arXiv 2605.24660) / "99% Success Paradox" (arXiv 2605.18857)** — catalog-size/collapse-zone analysis relevant to the phase-transition hypothesis.

### Novelty verdict by claimed contribution

**(i) Pre-deployment prediction of marginal degradation from an unseen tool addition — GENUINELY OPEN, WITH CAVEATS.** A targeted search found no work that learns a feature-based regressor mapping candidate-tool features to a predicted degradation delta without executing the post-addition workload. LLMCert-T is nearest and does it by execution/sampling. This is your defensible core — but it is "open" largely because it is a straightforward transfer of datamodels to a new object, so the novelty is incremental, not conceptual.

**(ii) Predicting the resulting tool-selection distribution shift (KL/JS/TV/Wasserstein) — WEAK NOVELTY.** Distribution-shift quantification with these exact metrics is standard in LLM analysis and drift monitoring, and predicting a shift from an intervention is what datamodels already do. Reframing the target as a distribution rather than a scalar is a nice touch, not a contribution by itself.

**(iii) Structural interaction model that explains the prediction — LARGELY PRE-EMPTED.** HYSET already builds a learned structural model with pairwise + higher-order (cardinality-specific) interactions over tools that generalizes to held-out tools. Your only differentiator is the target: degradation-caused-by-an-addition vs. HYSET's set-utility-for-retrieval. Thin, but not zero.

### Practical assessment (tractability, effect size, cost, cheap baseline)

- **Effect size and stability.** The degradation effect is real and often large (7.59%–85.58% in LongFuncEval; 4–51 tools taking calendar accuracy 43%→2%; 13.62%→43.13% retrieval swings), which is good for signal. But it is highly regime-dependent and confounded by prompt sensitivity, tool ordering ("lost in the middle"), and seed variance. Predicting a *marginal* delta from a single addition is harder than predicting aggregate degradation, because the marginal effect is often small relative to run-to-run variance until you cross the collapse zone. Expect low signal-to-noise for individual additions in the safe zone (<~15 tools) and larger, more predictable effects near/after the cliff. This is a genuine tractability risk.
- **Compute/data cost.** The core grid — 50 tools, 20 task families, 5 models, 100 candidate additions, 10 seeds — implies on the order of 10^5 labeled agent runs for the "with-addition" condition alone (5 × 100 × 20 × 10 ≈ 100,000), plus baselines. At multi-step agent trajectories, this is meaningful but not prohibitive for a small lab — plausibly low-thousands to low-tens-of-thousands of USD if most of the grid runs on small/open models and frontier models are reserved for a generalization slice. **Latency, not dollars, is likely the binding constraint.** A scaled version (hundreds of tools, real MCP servers) should reuse MCPEvol-Bench / MCP-Bench harnesses rather than build a new one.
- **The cheap-baseline threat is the crux — and the proposal is right to flag it as a falsification criterion.** Because degradation is driven largely by semantic/lexical overlap and redundancy (exactly what ToolScope and Semantic Tool Discovery exploit), a plain baseline — cosine similarity between the candidate tool's description embedding and its nearest neighbors in the catalog, plus catalog size — will likely capture a large fraction of the predictable variance. If a two-feature baseline (max/mean neighbor cosine + catalog size) reaches within a few points of your learned interference model's predictive R²/AUC, contribution (iii) is deflated and the paper's value shrinks to "we built the benchmark and showed the cheap baseline is strong" — publishable, but as a measurement/negative result, not a method.

## Recommendations

**Stage 0 — Falsify first (~1–2 weeks, cheap).** Build a *small* counterfactual dataset: 1–2 open models, ~30 tools, ~5 task families, ~50 candidate additions, ~5 seeds. Measure the true marginal delta (wrong-tool rate + task success) per addition. Then test whether the cheap baseline (candidate-vs-catalog embedding cosine + catalog size) already predicts it. Decision thresholds:
- If the cheap baseline explains most of the reliably-predictable variance (its R² is within ~0.05 of a strong learned model, or the incremental AUC from schema/interaction features is not significant across seeds): **do not** pursue the structural-model paper — pivot to the benchmark/measurement framing below.
- If there is a clear, reproducible gap that structural features (schema overlap, field/enum overlap, pairwise interference terms) close: proceed to Stage 1.

**Stage 1 — Core paper (if Stage 0 validates).** Scale to the ~50 × 20 × 5 grid. Frame the one defensible contribution precisely: "a feature-based ex-ante predictor of the marginal degradation caused by adding an *unseen* tool, evaluated for zero-shot generalization to unseen tools/families/catalogs/models." Position explicitly against datamodels ("we transfer the datamodel idea from training data to tool catalogs"), LLMCert-T ("prediction without execution vs. their sampling-based certification"), and HYSET ("degradation-of-addition target vs. their set-retrieval target"). Report the cheap baseline as a first-class competitor, not a strawman.

**Stage 2 — Artifact.** Release an open benchmark — "predict the marginal degradation of a candidate tool addition" — reusing MCPEvol-Bench / MCP-Bench servers. Even if the method only modestly beats the baseline, a clean reusable benchmark plus the honest baseline finding is a solid workshop paper and an excellent portfolio piece demonstrating causal-eval, agent-harness, and datamodeling engineering skill.

**Form recommendation.** This is a strong **engineering portfolio project** and a plausible **workshop paper** (agents or eval workshop). It is a borderline **main-conference** paper only if Stage 0/1 show the learned interference model clearly beats the cheap baseline AND you demonstrate zero-shot cross-catalog/cross-model generalization. Do **not** frame it as a broad new theory of tool interference — that ground is taken. Frame it narrowly as "ex-ante, execution-free prediction of an unseen tool's marginal damage," the one slot that is empirically open.

**Strongest reframing if pure novelty is weak.** Pivot from "predict the scalar delta" to "predict *which* additions trigger the phase-transition/collapse and *which pairs* interfere" — a screening/triage tool for MCP registries that flags high-risk additions for online A/B testing (your "quarantine" idea). This is decision-useful even at modest predictive accuracy, aligns with the LLMCert-T gating narrative while filling its predictive gap, and fits the "expose only if predicted risk < threshold, else quarantine" workflow.

## Caveats
- Several cited 2026 arXiv preprints are very recent and unrefereed; some contain forward-dated model names (e.g., "GPT-5.4," "Claude-Sonnet-4-6"), a marker of possibly speculative or synthetic content. Treat single-preprint claims as provisional and verify before citing in a submission.
- The "open" verdict for contribution (i) is a bounded negative-search result — absence of evidence, not proof no such work exists. The field is moving fast (multiple directly-adjacent papers appeared mid-2026), so a direct predecessor could surface or be published during your project. Re-run the prior-art sweep at submission time.
- Effect-size predictability is the biggest technical risk: the marginal effect of a single addition may be dominated by seed/prompt variance in the safe-zone regime, making the core quantity hard to predict precisely regardless of features. Stage 0 exists specifically to detect this before you spend the compute budget.
